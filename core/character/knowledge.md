Knowledge represents what your character *knows*, as distinct from what they can *do*. A Skill answers "can you pull this off" and takes a roll because failure is a real possibility - a Knowledge answers "do you recognize this," and recognition doesn't have a meaningful failure state. When you want to recall, recognize, interpret, or deduce something, the GM checks the Knowledge whose question fits and simply tells you what your tier reveals. No die is ever rolled against a Knowledge.

---

## The Three Tiers

Every Knowledge subject sits at one of three tiers for your character:

- **None:** You're guessing, same as anyone off the street. The GM tells you nothing beyond what's plainly observable, or answers only through an in-fiction source (a book, an NPC, a Regional Lore-holding ally).
- **Familiarity:** You know the subject in broad strokes - enough to recognize what you're looking at, place it in context, and know there's more to it than you personally know. The GM answers common-knowledge questions freely and can gate genuinely obscure or specialist-level detail behind a roleplay hook (finding the right book, asking the right expert) rather than a die roll.
- **Expertise:** You could teach this subject at an institutional level - a university chair, a guild master, a court's own authority. The GM answers almost anything you ask, including the obscure and the specialist. Even Expertise isn't omniscience: a subject as broad as History or Arcane Theory always has some fact, some source, some corner that even its foremost scholars haven't found - the GM can still say "you don't know that one" without it being a failure of the system.

**Why no roll:** knowing a fact isn't a contest of skill under pressure the way swinging a sword or picking a lock is - it's binary, and a binary question doesn't need a randomizer. Rolling to fail at recalling something your character should plausibly know is the single most common complaint that led to this system: the GM either concedes and tells you anyway (making the roll pointless) or holds the line and your character is made inexplicably dumb for a round of bad luck. Knowledge sidesteps the whole problem - ask "are you familiar with X," check the tier, done.

---

## Choosing Knowledge

Knowledge is chosen once, at character creation (see [[Character Creation|character_creation]]'s Step 6) - there is no per-level Knowledge budget. A character can still gain new Knowledge through play at the GM's discretion (a story beat, an apprenticeship, a season spent in a library), but that's a narrative reward the GM hands out, not a mechanical progression track.

**Cost per tier:**

| Tier | Cost |
| :---- | :---: |
| Familiarity | 1 point |
| Expertise | 3 points total (2 more, once you're already Familiar) |

Expertise costs disproportionately more than Familiarity on purpose - "knows the broad strokes" and "could teach it at an institutional level" shouldn't be a linear difference.

**Where points come from:**

- **Freebie Pool:** equal to your **MIND score at character creation**, minimum 0 (snapshotted at the end of Step 5 - it does not grow if MIND rises later from leveling), spendable on *any* Knowledge subject with no restriction. This is what you picked up on your own, out of personal curiosity, independent of what your people taught you or what your job demanded - a MIND 0 or lower character gets none of it, same as they get no literacy below MIND 2; a character who invested in MIND for spellcasting or reasoning gets a real payoff in how broadly read they are too.
- **Race:** grants a small number of points restricted to a curated list of subjects (noted in that race's own entry in [[Races|races_overview]]) - what your people generally pass down.
- **Career:** grants a small number of points restricted to a curated list of subjects (noted in that career's own entry in [[Careers|careers]]) - what the work itself taught you. Free, same as a Career's Skill Bonuses - neither draws from the Freebie Pool.

Points from different sources can be spent on the same subject, climbing it from None toward Familiarity toward Expertise rather than the later grant being wasted - a Career granting 1 point in Physic and the Freebie Pool adding another both land in the same subject, reaching Familiarity together. If a subject is already at Expertise, further points aimed at it are simply wasted - there's no tier above Expertise to climb into.

A maxed-out character stacking every source into the same handful of subjects should land around 1-2 subjects at Expertise plus 3-5 more at Familiarity - a genuinely learned person, not an encyclopedia. With 24 subjects on the list below, that's still a small fraction of everything there is to know.

## The Knowledge Pool

### Learned Knowledge

*The disciplines of the university, the monastery, and the chancery. Most require literacy to acquire.*

**Arcane Theory**
The study of magic as the physics it is: the Five Laws, the five arcane schools, the mechanics of sympathy and connection, and how enchantment anchors borrowed power into a vessel. You need not be able to cast a single spell to understand precisely what a caster can, cannot, and should not do.

**Theology**
Scripture, liturgy, the hierarchy and disputes of the faiths, and the doctrine of the five Faith schools. This is also the discipline of interpreting what the gods actually say - prophecy and signs arrive in symbol and fragment, and reading them rightly is a scholar's art with a long history of being wrong.

**Signature Lore**
Every working of magic leaves residue - on the place where it was cast, and on the body of the one who cast it. This is the forensic art of reading both: naming the school from a scar or a scorch, judging how recently and how recklessly power was used, and recognizing the telltale *absence* left behind when someone tried to scrub the evidence.

**Thaumatic Law**
The legal machinery surrounding magic: licensing, prohibition, which offenses fall to church courts and which to the crown, and what the accused can expect from each. Knowing what a caster did is one skill; knowing what will be done *to* them is this one.

**Physic**
The learned medicine of the body - humors, anatomy, diagnosis, and treatment. It includes the quieter, grayer trade of easing or concealing the marks that magic leaves on flesh, a service that is rarely legal and never cheap.

**Alchemical Theory**
The properties and transformation of substances: distillation, compounds, acids, poisons, and powders - including the black powder that is changing the shape of war. The alchemist knows what things are made of and what they can be made into.

**Natural Philosophy**
The workings of the mundane world: the elements, the motions of things, the natures of beasts, plants, and stones. Where Arcane Theory explains what the Hydromancer did to the river, Natural Philosophy explains the river.

**Astrology & Cosmology**
The reckoning of the heavens - calendars, seasons, the catalog of what the sky normally does - and the crucial art of noticing when it does something it shouldn't. The stars see nothing themselves, but the gods write signs upon them, and telling a genuine sign from a comet, or from a god's forgery in another god's script, is this discipline's highest and least certain work.

**Letters & Rhetoric**
Logic, composition, disputation, and the classical authors. In an age of printing presses, the ability to draft a contract, or write the pamphlet that sets a city alight is no idle accomplishment.

### Worldly Knowledge

*The knowledge of courts, roads, and counting-houses.*

**History**
What is recorded of the past: dynasties, wars, treaties, and chronicles. Remember that magic cannot reach what no mind remembers and no page records - the historian's craft is not a poor substitute for divination, it is the only door to the past that exists.

**Genealogy & Heraldry**
Bloodlines, coats of arms, precedence, succession, and the courtesies owed to each. This is the knowledge of *who someone is* - what they are owed, what they may claim, and who stands between them and what they want.

**Statecraft & Intrigue**
The living map of power: factions, alliances, the conventions of diplomacy and spycraft, and the currents moving beneath the surface of any court. History tells you how the duchy was won; this tells you who is about to lose it.

**Geography & Cartography**
Realms, routes, borders, and distant lands - and the maps that describe them, along with a healthy sense of which maps can be trusted.

**Commerce**
Coinage, exchange, credit, guilds, and the worth of goods. The knowledge of how money moves, and of what anything - from a bolt of cloth to an heirloom blade with fading enchantment - will actually fetch.

---

### Common Knowledge

*The knowledge of the village, the road, and the alley. No book required.*

**Husbandry & Agriculture**
Crops, seasons, soil, and stock. Whether the land will yield, whether the beast is sound, and what the countryside needs to survive the winter.

**Folklore & Superstition**
What the common folk hold true: omens, wards, saints' days, and local custom. Much of it is wrong. Some of it is old truth the learned world forgot or suppressed - and telling the difference is rarely possible from the outside.

**Streetwise**
The customs of the underworld: cant, fencing, smuggling, and who runs what. In a world where a lock of hair is a weapon, this includes the sympathy-trade - who buys a lord's comb, who sells cut hair, and what a link to a well-guarded target costs.

**Regional Lore** *(may be taken multiple times)*
Deep familiarity with one specific region: its people, politics, roads, legends, and dangers. Each time you take this Knowledge, choose a different region.

---

### Martial & Technical Knowledge

*The knowledge of war, works, and the wright's bench.*

**Warfare**
How armies fight, march, eat, and fall apart: tactics, logistics, mercenary custom, and the new doctrine of pike and shot.

**Engineering & Mechanisms**
How things are built, held, and brought down: fortification, artillery, mining, mills, pumps, locks, and clockwork. The same knowledge that raises a wall knows where it will break.

**Seafaring & Navigation**
Charts, stars, tides, winds, ship types, and the customs of ports. How the sea is crossed by those who intend to come back.

**Crafts & Trades**
The work of the guilds: materials, methods, and quality. The knowledge of whether a thing is well-made, how it was made, and what that says about who made it.

---

### Dark Knowledge

*What is hunted, and what is hidden.*

**Bestiary Lore**
The habits, signs, and weaknesses of the creatures that haunt the world. Natural Philosophy knows the wolf; this knows what hunts the wolf.

**Forbidden Lore**
The truths that were removed from the record: the remnants of Dzinari magic, the origins of the Mystik races, what Subjugation actually is, and what the great institutions each decline to publish. Its subject is not one field but one property - suppression. This knowledge is dangerous to hold, and more dangerous to be known to hold.
