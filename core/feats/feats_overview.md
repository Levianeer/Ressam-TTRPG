Feats represent specialized training, natural talents, or hard-won experience. Characters begin with 2 feats at 1st level and gain an additional feat at 4th, 6th, 9th, and 12th level.

**Prerequisites & Bonuses:**  
Racial and Career skill bonuses DO count toward feat prerequisites \- not only invested skill ranks.

### **How high a prerequisite is allowed to go**

Attributes come from a standard array now ([[Assign Attributes|character_creation]]) and grow by exactly \+1 at Levels 4, 8 and 12, so a prerequisite number is really a **level gate in disguise** - but which level depends on which priority slot you gave the Attribute in question, not just your Feats priority. `DESIGN_GUIDE.md`'s 5e mirror treats a Ressam Attribute as a straight stand-in for a 5th Edition ability *modifier*, so that's shown alongside each rung below as the sanity check:

| Priority given to this Attribute | Level 1 | Level 4 | Level 8 | Level 12 |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **A** | 3 (5e +3) | 4 (+4) | 5 (+5) | 5 (+5, capped) |
| **B** or **C** | 2 (+2) | 3 (+3) | 4 (+4) | 5 (+5) |
| **D** or **E** | 1 (+1) | 2 (+2) | 3 (+3) | 4 (+4) |

**D and E never reach 5 in that Attribute through leveling alone** - three bumps on a 1 tops out at 4 by Level 12. That's the real price of a dumped Attribute (see [[Attribute Limits|character_creation]]), not an oversight to patch. **A Skill's Rank can never exceed its governing Attribute**, so a rank requirement is an Attribute requirement too: `\+3 ranks in Athletics` cannot be met before STR 3 - Level 4 if STR was a B/C priority, Level 8 if it was D or E.

Three rules keep a prerequisite honest, and every Feat in this chapter is written to them:

- **No ordinary Feat asks for an Attribute above 2.** A 2 is a 5e \+2 modifier - already a full step past the \+1 (a 13) that's the ceiling on every prerequisite 5e itself ever writes. It's in hand at Level 1 for a B/C-prioritized Attribute, Level 4 for a D/E one - either way, the Feat slot itself is the real cost, and there are only six of those across twelve levels at a **C** priority.
- **No Feat asks for more than \+3 ranks in a Skill.** A rank of 3 means the Attribute is 3 - a 5e \+3, two steps past that same \+1 ceiling - in hand at Level 4 for a B/C-prioritized Attribute, itself a Feat-grant level. A D/E-prioritized Attribute doesn't reach 3 until Level 8, which grants no Feat of its own (Feats land at 4, 6, 9, and 12 - Attribute bumps at 4, 8, and 12 are a separate schedule) - but the stat is already sitting there waiting by Level 9, the next slot that opens. That is the intended ceiling for "this is a mastery pick," and it is a level gate, not an Attribute gate.
- **A Prestige Feat may ask for an Attribute of 3, and no more.** Prestige Feats unlock at Level 5, and a 3 is already in hand by then for a B/C-prioritized Attribute (Level 4), with a D/E one close behind (Level 8). Asking for 4 would put the whole category out of reach until Level 8 for a B/C Attribute, and Level 12 - the very end of the track - for a D/E one, which is not what "unlocks at Level 5" means.

**If a Feat needs to be harder to get, make it need two different things, not one bigger number.** A second Skill at \+2, a specific weapon group, a Race, a spell already known - all of these cost a real build decision without pushing the Feat past a level the player can reach.

**General, Martial, and Skill Feats spread across three rungs, not one flat bar.** A single ceiling (everything at \+2 ranks or Attribute 2\) reads as no curve at all - every Feat costing the same makes the prerequisite decorative. Instead:

- **\+1 rank in a Skill, or Attribute 1+, for a broadly-available "starter" pick** - reachable well before Level 1's array even finishes being assigned around a concept.
- **\+2 ranks, or Attribute 2, for the default** - the ordinary-Feat ceiling above, and where most Feats should sit.
- **\+3 ranks in a Skill for a genuine mastery pick** - the higher ceiling the second rule above already sanctions, paired with a stronger Benefit than the \+1/\+2 rungs get, so the harder prerequisite is buying something, not just gatekeeping. A bare Attribute prerequisite cannot reach this rung on an ordinary Feat - route a mastery-tier Feat through a Skill instead, or add a second requirement per the paragraph above.

## Feat Categories

### **General Feats**

General feats are available to all characters and represent broadly applicable abilities that don't require specialized combat or magical training.

### **Martial Feats**

Martial feats are how a martial character's toolkit actually expands - see [[Martial Feats|martial_feats]]. Real weapon-mastery techniques and battlefield tactics, each one a keyword ability a Feat slot buys outright - the opposite of a Magic Feat's access tax (below).

### **Magic Feats**

Magic Feats are one unified system across all ten schools - see [[Magic Feats|magic_feats]]. Each school offers exactly four Feats, Novice/Adept/Expert/Master, each one only access to that tier's difficulty of working - an access tax, not a toolkit expansion the way a Martial Feat is.

### **Skill Feats**

Skill feats represent mastery of specific non-combat abilities and specialized training.

### **Prestige Feats**

Prestige feats represent fundamental transformations in how a character interacts with the world, gated behind Level 5 and a narrative ritual rather than raw prerequisites - see `prestige_feats.md` for the full requirements.

### **Racial Feats**

Racial feats are gated behind a specific race instead of a Skill or Attribute score - a trait strong enough to cost a Feat slot rather than come free with that race at character creation.
