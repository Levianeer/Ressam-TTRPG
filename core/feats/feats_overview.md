Feats represent specialized training, natural talents, or hard-won experience. Characters begin with 2 feats at 1st level and gain an additional feat at 4th, 6th, 9th, and 12th level.

**Prerequisites & Bonuses:**  
Racial and Career skill bonuses DO count toward feat prerequisites \- not only invested skill ranks.

### **How high a prerequisite is allowed to go**

Attributes come from a standard array now ([[Assign Attributes|character_creation]]) and grow by exactly \+1 at Levels 4, 8 and 12, so a prerequisite number is really a **level gate in disguise**. Outside an **A** priority, an Attribute of 2 is where you start, 3 arrives at Level 4, 4 at Level 8, and 5 at Level 12. **A Skill's Rank can never exceed its governing Attribute**, so a rank requirement is an Attribute requirement too: `\+3 ranks in Athletics` cannot be met before STR 3.

Three rules keep a prerequisite honest, and every Feat in this chapter is written to them:

- **No ordinary Feat asks for an Attribute above 2.** Two is a number any character who cared about that Attribute has at Level 1. This is the whole gate - the Feat slot itself is the real cost, and there are only six of those across twelve levels at a **C** priority.
- **No Feat asks for more than \+3 ranks in a Skill.** A rank of 3 means the Attribute is 3, which means Level 4 for most characters - the level a Feat is granted at anyway. That is the intended ceiling for "this is a mastery pick," and it is a level gate, not an Attribute gate.
- **A Prestige Feat may ask for an Attribute of 3, and no more.** Prestige Feats unlock at Level 5; a 3 is in hand at Level 4. Asking for 4 would put the whole category out of reach until Level 8 for four of the five priority letters, which is not what "unlocks at Level 5" means.

**If a Feat needs to be harder to get, make it need two different things, not one bigger number.** A second Skill at \+2, a specific weapon group, a Race, a spell already known - all of these cost a real build decision without pushing the Feat past a level the player can reach.

## Feat Categories

### **General Feats**

General feats are available to all characters and represent broadly applicable abilities that don't require specialized combat or magical training.

### **Martial Feats**

Martial feats represent combat prowess and battlefield tactics. These feats focus on weapon mastery, defensive techniques, and physical combat superiority.

### **Magic Feats**

Magic Feats are one unified system across all ten schools - see [[Magic Feats|magic_feats]]. Each school offers a Novice/Adept/Expert/Master **Unlock** (access to that tier's difficulty of working), a matching **Focus** (built around Signature Workings), and **Combination** Feats that grant workings spanning two or more schools.

### **Skill Feats**

Skill feats represent mastery of specific non-combat abilities and specialized training.

### **Prestige Feats**

Prestige feats represent fundamental transformations in how a character interacts with the world, gated behind Level 5 and a narrative ritual rather than raw prerequisites - see `prestige_feats.md` for the full requirements.

### **Racial Feats**

Racial feats are gated behind a specific race instead of a Skill or Attribute score - a trait strong enough to cost a Feat slot rather than come free with that race at character creation.
