# The Laws of Magic

Every spell, ritual, miracle, and minor working in Ressam operates under a fixed set of laws. They are not mortal doctrine; they are the physics of magic, as fixed as gravity and as indifferent to intent. Scholars of Mafsoleios call them the Constants. The Fe Alada calls them the Design.

This document exists for one purpose: when the spell lists are silent, the Laws decide. Any effect a caster attempts can be checked against them. If it violates a Law, it does not happen - not "it fails," not "it costs more," it does not happen, the same way a stone does not fall upward. Everything that does not violate a Law is a question of price, covered under the Three Costs below.

---

## The Five Laws

### I. The Law of Borrowed Substance

*Nothing is ever made. Everything is borrowed.*

Magic does not create matter, it condenses energy into the temporary shape of matter. Conjured stone is real stone while the magic holds it; conjured water quenches real thirst. But condensed substance is a loan, and it is always collected: unattended, conjured matter thins and unravels back into the energy it was drawn from. This is why no caster has ever conjured a fortune, fed a famine, or raised a fortress from nothing - the gold fades, the bread reverts, the tower stands only as long as its maker keeps paying for it.

**Anchoring.** The loan can be stretched, not forgiven. Magic bound into a physical vessel (worked into steel, fired into clay, woven into cloth) decays at the pace of that vessel rather than the pace of a caster's attention. This is the mechanism behind enchantment: not creation, but slow, anchored borrowing. An enchanted item still fails eventually, over centuries rather than hours, its magic thinning until it stops functioning.

### II. The Law of the Present

*Magic touches what is. Never what was. Never what will be.*

No magic reverses a moment, halts one, or reaches one that has not yet arrived. True divination reads the present with unusual thoroughness: what exists now, what is written now, what is remembered now. A skilled diviner can appear to predict the future the way a skilled physician can predict a death - both are reading present conditions with precision, not seeing forward in time. The unrecorded past is equally sealed: what no mind remembers and no surface records is beyond magic's reach.

**Exception: prophecy.** The gods are not bound by this Law. When prophecy enters the world, it arrives as a granted vision, not a cast spell - no formula produces it, and no caster commands it. Divine sight is delivered in symbol and fragment, and should not be trusted as literal. Prophecy exists in Ressam. It is never a resource a caster can access on demand.

### III. The Law of the Soul

*Magic cannot create a soul, destroy a soul, or grow a soul.*

The soul is the one substance magic did not make and cannot unmake. It can be touched, held, moved, or imprisoned, since it already exists and magic may act on what exists. But no working has ever kindled a new soul, ended one, or caused one to develop beyond what it was. A soul changes only by living, and living is the one thing no spell provides.

This is why necromancy is a craft of bodies, not people. The corpse moves; the person does not return. An empire once animated the dead at continental scale for centuries, and in that time no soul returned through that door. It does not open from this side.

**The Mafsoleios exception.** Mafsoleios built its practice on the edge of this Law rather than past it. Their rite does not restore the departed; it pins a soul in the moment before its final departure and fastens it back to its body. The result matches the Law's prediction exactly: the pinned retain the memory, personality, and unfinished business they held at the moment of death, and develop no further. Mafsoleios calls this preservation.

Above this Law stand the gods only. Reconstituting a soul scattered into primordial energy is beyond mortal art. True restoration exists solely as divine miracle, precisely because no mortal casting can perform it.

### IV. The Law of Essential Nature

*Mortal craft alters what a thing does. Only will alters what a thing is.*

Everything has a nature: what it is beneath any applied effect. Spellcraft, arcane or divine, acts on state, not nature. It heats stone without making the stone stop being stone, enrages a person without changing who they are, disguises a wolf while leaving the wolf intact beneath the disguise. Every transformation produced by formula or prayer is temporary; the underlying nature reasserts itself once the working lapses.

**Exception: willed beings.** Some beings were not born but willed into existence by powers that did not consult this Law when they made them. The Mystik races are this exception given flesh - their nature was overwritten at creation by an act of will, which is why aspects of it can be altered again by will in turn. The school of Subjugation is a surviving fragment of the Dzinari magic that touched identity at this level, not merely its surface. This is why Subjugation reads as categorically different from other schools of magic: it is not a tenth school built on the same Law as the rest, it is a narrow exception to the Law worked by practitioners who do not fully understand what they are using.

For everyone and everything else, the Law holds without exception.

### V. The Law of Connection

*Magic requires a thread. No thread, no working.*

Power does not cross distance unaided. To affect a target, a caster must be connected to it by sight, touch, or sympathy. Sight is the most common thread: what the eye holds, the will can reach. Touch is direct contact. Sympathy is the third and most exploitable: a part standing for the whole - blood, hair, or a possession carried against the skin for years retains a faint connection to its former owner, which a skilled practitioner can work along.

There is no fourth thread. A name, a description, or intent, however strong, is not a connection. No caster teleports without traversing the distance between two points, and no seer locates a stranger through effort alone. This is why the cautious burn their cut hair and the powerful are buried in secret: to be scried upon or struck at range, something of the target must first be taken. The theft, not the spell, is the vulnerable step.

## The Three Costs

The Five Laws state what can never happen. The Three Costs govern what is lawful but priced, and that price cannot be negotiated away, only paid.

### Conservation

Magic redirects more easily than it imposes. Fanning an existing flame, deepening an existing shadow, or accelerating a wound the body is already closing works with the world's current state, and costs less. Conjuring fire in the rain, light in total darkness, or vigor in a dying body works against the world's current state, and costs more. The further an effect stands from what the environment was already doing, the more it demands of the caster forcing it.

**Healing.** Healing magic sits under Conservation. The body heals; magic does not. What a healing spell provides is borrowed flesh pressed over a wound and a slowed rate of blood loss, buying time rather than producing recovery. It is condensed substance like any other conjuration, so the First Law applies to it: the borrowed flesh fades, and either the body has finished its own repair by then, or the wound remains. Only rest, food, and time produce actual recovery.

### Signature

All magic leaves residue at the location it was performed: a trace that trained senses can detect for hours afterward, indicating what was done, roughly when, and by what school. There is no untraceable use of magic, only investigators who arrive too late or lack the training to read the trace.

**Shadowmancy.** The school of shadow is built to dampen this residue, not eliminate it - dimming, softening, and thinning the signature it would otherwise leave. It cannot eliminate it, because concealment is itself a working and leaves its own trace. A location scrubbed by shadow magic reads as an anomaly: an unnatural absence of residue where some should exist. To an untrained observer this looks like nothing. To a trained one, it is a signature specific to that school.

### Reciprocity

Arcane magic is paid for in advance, in study, materials, and precise formulae. Divine magic is paid for continuously: power granted by a god comes with that god's ongoing judgment and awareness attached. A miracle is a favor, not a tool, and a favor is tracked. A caster who drifts from their deity's doctrine finds their petitions answered more slowly, then reluctantly, then not at all - not as direct punishment, but because the source of that power is a relationship, and a relationship that lapses stops producing results. Divine magic cannot exist without an active relationship, and that relationship carries obligations in both directions.

## Adjudication: When the Books Are Silent

The spell lists cannot anticipate every intention a caster will have. The Laws can. When a novel effect is attempted, evaluate it in this order:

1. **Does it cross a Law?** If the attempt creates permanent substance from nothing, touches the past or future, creates or grows a soul, rewrites essential nature by mortal craft, or reaches a target with no thread of connection, it cannot happen. Do not soften this to "very difficult" - the Laws describe what the world permits, not an obstacle of scale. A caster attempting the impossible achieves nothing and spends whatever resources they committed finding that out. Holding this line without exception is what keeps every other ruling predictable.
2. **Is it small?** If the effect is brief, minor, and thematically consistent with the caster's art, treat it as a minor working and price it accordingly.
3. **Otherwise, it is a spell, and spells are priced, not refused.** Evaluate a lawful but substantial effect the way any other spell is evaluated: what it does, what it targets, how it is delivered, then weigh it against the Three Costs. Is it working with the world's current state or against it? How much residue will it leave? What relationship, if any, is being drawn on? A lawful but ambitious attempt is never answered with "no." It is answered with a price, and it is on the caster to determine whether they can pay it.

Applied consistently, these three questions keep magic in Ressam bounded and discoverable rather than arbitrary.
