# Technology

Ressam sits at a matchlock-and-plate baseline, and it has held there for four hundred years. This is not a ceiling anything enforces \- no Law of Magic caps invention, no god forbids the wheel turning further. It is a historical fact with historical causes: the mortal world's most advanced civilizations were annihilated rather than merely defeated, and nothing since has been left alone long enough to climb back past where that annihilation cut them off.

---

## The Sundering's Verdict

Every nation the God-Dragons' wrath touched was left "ash and bone," but not every ashed nation left survivors. The ones that did not were, disproportionately, the ones that had pushed furthest \- the great pre-Sundering powers with the deepest guild lineages, the largest standing faculties, the longest unbroken chains of master teaching apprentice. When the dragons finished with them, they finished completely: to the last man, woman, and child, with no one left to carry what they knew out of the ruins.

This matters more than the raw destruction. A city can be rebuilt from rubble by people who remember how it was built. A guild's forge secrets, a university's accumulated proofs, a navy's logistics doctrine cannot be rebuilt by anyone, because they were never written down in a form that outlives the people holding them \- they lived in hands and in memory, and the hands and memory were the thing the dragons erased. The Third Age's nations are not a slowed continuation of that lost peak. They descend from the lesser, less advanced survivor populations who happened to still be alive to crawl from the wreckage, rebuilding from whatever fragments those particular survivors personally carried. The peak itself has no heirs.

Mafsoleios' Grand Gnosion and the Fellowship of Saint Cadogan both exist because someone, eventually, drew the obvious lesson: knowledge that lives only in living memory dies exactly once.

---

## No Century Left Alone

A technological leap needs surplus, and surplus needs decades without a war eating it. The Third Age has not produced those decades. The one period that came closest \- the Great Arms Race (50-200 RC), when the reconstructing nations pushed hardest against each other's expanding militaries \- ended not in a settled peace but in interruption, swallowed by the Orkhaden Resurgence's pressure on every border at once. Nothing since has fared better. By 402 RC, seven wars run concurrently across the continent, and every nation named in them is spending this generation's surplus on this generation's war rather than banking it toward the next 'tier of craft'.

This is the ordinary mechanism, and it is sufficient on its own. No nation in Ressam has failed to invent the next thing because the next thing is impossible. Each has simply never had the fifty uninterrupted years that inventing it would cost.

---

## The Coalition's Long Game

One nation is quietly getting those years. The Tagarian Coalition's list of open wars is short next to everyone else's \- the Eshzera War against the Greater Groves, ongoing friction with the Orkhaden Horde \- and neither has cost it a continental front. What the Coalition has spent the last century doing instead is colonizing: its hold on the coastal islands of Aurkhan and its footholds in Gelidia are not the leftovers of some past war, they are ongoing, deliberate expansion. Territory taken this way does not spend a nation's surplus the way war does. It adds to it \- new ports, new timber, new hulls in the water, new coin flowing back through Mar Scrips before a rival navy even notices the flag has changed.

The fleet that expansion has bought is second only to the Holy Reino Empire's by raw count, but built of ships that already dwarf the Empire's in size, and increasingly in sophistication \- arguably the single most capable navy on the water, even if not yet the largest. The Coalition is not ahead of Tor'Az on the ladder the rest of this chapter describes; Tor'Az's mechanized war machines remain the continent's true ceiling for now, and nothing here changes that. But Tor'Az's lead was bought by a century spent defending an isolated stronghold, while the Coalition's growth was bought by a century spent adding territory instead of holding a line. Of every great power in Ressam, the Coalition is the one actually accumulating the uninterrupted peacetime a real leap requires. If any nation closes the gap with Tor'Az, or opens a new one nobody else can follow, this book's money is on Felnis before Karak's Spine.

---

## What Guilds Keep, Faith Directs

Two more forces shape the climb even where war doesn't.

**Guild secrecy.** Tor'Az Triumvirate holds the largest technological lead of any nation on the continent, and treats that lead as the one resource it will never share. Its doctrine is not merely cautious, it is closed: no outsider learns what the deep halls of Karak's Spine actually produce and lives to publish it. A lead that never crosses a border cannot lift anyone else's baseline, and the Triumvirate's entire foreign policy \- arming both sides of wars it has no stake in, guarding Tradehaven as its one deliberate crack in an otherwise sealed door \- is built to keep that lead exactly that isolated. It is also not unanimous even at home: Terrevault's orthodox guilds regard Dromgadol's mechanized creations with open theological hostility, a fight the Triumvirate has never fully settled.

**Doctrinal continuity.** The Holy Reino Empire runs on the opposite mechanism, and it is the one real exception to "the peak has no heirs." Tempeste, one of the four God-Dragons, wore a human face through the Sundering and preached under it as Saint Maarkest, founding what began as the Dragon Cult in secret to spread word of the dragons' return. No mortal in Ressam has ever confirmed that Maarkest and Tempeste were one and the same \- Maarkest always denied any hand in the Sundering, and even among the gods it is only ever suspicion that the Saint lied, never certainty. That cult never died with the Sundering the way every guild and university elsewhere did \- it grew into the Fe Alada, and the Fe Alada became the literal foundation the Gran Reino Empire was built on top of, not merely a faith practiced within it. The High Lord in Gran Reino rules as emperor and pope in the same breath because the two offices were never separate to begin with. That unbroken line, running from a God-Dragon's own hand through four centuries, makes the Empire technology's actual inheritor: not the deepest pre-Sundering guild lineage, since that belonged to nations left with no survivors at all, but the only nation whose founding institution never had to be rebuilt from zero. The Fe Alada does not reject new craft, it ritually sanctions it \- the Portatores' faith-bound plate is that process working as intended, not an exception carved out of it. The cost is that nothing in the Empire advances without the Church's approval first, which keeps the Empire a step behind the Coalition's unregulated colonial growth. But the Empire is the one nation close enough behind the Coalition to matter, and the only great power still advancing on inherited footing rather than from scratch.

## The Shared Baseline

What every nation has, regardless of who leads: matchlock and wheellock firearms, full plate and its lighter derivatives, black powder, sail, and craft production organized around guild workshops rather than any larger unit. Literacy and record-keeping are real \- the Grand Gnosion is the largest library in the world precisely because most knowledge elsewhere is still guarded, scattered, or unwritten \- but scholarship is a small, hoarded thing, not a mass institution. This is the floor every nation in Ressam stands on. Nobody has fallen below it since the Age of Revival; nobody has broken meaningfully above it either.

## The Hard Line

Nowhere in Ressam does steam power exist, or electricity, or interchangeable-parts mass manufacture, or a standing network for long-range communication. None of these are barred by anything magical \- the Laws of Magic have nothing to say about a steam engine, and nothing about Ressam's physics forbids one. The reason is the same three causes above, stacked: the population that might have reached them first was exterminated rather than slowed, no survivor nation has had the peacetime to close that gap since, and the one nation closest to closing it keeps its progress sealed behind guild silence and its own theological infighting. A GM introducing any of these into play should treat it as a genuine historical event \- a nation's turning point, a war's outcome \- not as background texture the setting already permits.

---

## A Different Axis

The Age of Mythos and its surviving fragments \- Dragon's Script chief among them \- are not a lost technological tier sitting above or below this baseline. They predate mortal craft entirely: the Great Artists' shaping of the world and the God-Dragons' own nature are acts of primordial magic, not engineering achievements a guild could have matched at any point in its history. A ruin inscribed with Dragon's Script is not a piece of superior machinery waiting to be reverse-engineered: it is a magical artifact from before technology, in the mortal sense, existed at all. Treat pre-Sundering mortal ruins (the lost peak of the section above) and Age of Mythos ruins (this section) as two different kinds of find with two different payoffs \- one is a fragment of a civilization that could, in principle, be rebuilt; the other is a fragment of something no civilization ever built.
