# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this repository is

This is **Ressam**, a tabletop RPG (TTRPG) rulebook written in Markdown. The repository is content/rules-authoring - edits to game design text, tables, and formulas, not software. Every file under `core/` is plain hand-authored Markdown, edited directly - there is no build step or generated content.

## Repository structure

- `core/core_rules.md` - foundational resolution mechanics: dice, attributes, skills, checks vs. Ward. Every roll is `1d12` plus one 0-5 number (a Skill Rank on a Check, an Attribute on a Ward), so the ceiling is 17 and 2 points of DC is exactly one step of one in six. The DC scale has four tiers - `5 / 7 / 9 / 11`, named Easy/Standard/Hard/Extreme - the same ladder `magic_overview.md`'s Lesser/Common/Greater/Legendary working tiers use, so both systems share one difficulty table. Situational modifiers stack on top of a tier, so real DCs in play climb well past 11. **A Ward takes a lower DC than a Skill Check** for the same fiction - set Wards one tier lower and keep unannounced hazards at Hard (9) or below, Extreme (11) reserved for named, telegraphed threats. Read this first; nearly every other chapter assumes these definitions. Two sibling chapters split off what used to live here once the single file grew too dense: `rest_and_survival.md` and `exploration/stealth_and_light.md`. Dashing and Disengage live in `positioning.md`; Grappling/Shoving/Disarming/Feint are Openings or attack types in `exchange.md`. **The `Contested Check` branch of Checks vs. Ward is currently unused** - kept explicitly labelled as an adjudication shape nothing calls, in case a future Feat or Feature needs it.
- `core/rest_and_survival.md` - the "logistics and consequences" chapter, split out of `core_rules.md`. Current shape: Carrying Capacity (one unified **Slots** pool, `6 + STR`) and Rest & Repair (Short/Field/Long, which also repairs armor's Dent/Rend/Guard lines directly) open the file, followed by Damage Types (Physical/Elemental/Occult, with Physical's three subtypes - Blunt/Piercing/Slashing - each carrying one real mechanical effect), Wounds & Death's Door (Named vs. Unnamed characters, Wound Penalty, **Downed**, Falling, Food and Water, Suffocation - healing restores real Wounds directly), Trauma (a shared **0-20** track with `magic_overview.md`, carrying no roll penalty of its own - it gates Scars and death only, and its main inflow is healing itself: every Wound healed costs 1 Trauma), **Revelry & Leisure** (the only way Trauma comes down), Scars (Trauma-band-triggered, with real mechanical effects - Flinching, Haunted, Hollow, Wary, Hair-Trigger, Palsied, Steeled - tested with a CHA Resolve check), and Conditions (the full status-effect table). **Two combat-adjacent systems live in `exchange.md` but are documented from here:** Fear (a creature's Fear rating triggers a Will-funded resist on approach) and Morale/Nerve (CHA-funded, Named characters take Trauma instead of Breaking). An unarmored target reads Dent 0 / Rend 5 like any other armor value, which lets a weak hit Turn Aside even unarmored.
- `core/character/` - building and advancing a character: `character_creation.md` (step-by-step guide), `attributes_and_skills.md` (reference glossary: what each of the 4 Attributes represents and what each Skill covers, grouped by category - the mechanics that use these numbers, like the Skill Check formula and DC tiers, stay in `core_rules.md` and are linked from here rather than duplicated), `knowledge.md` (what your character *knows*, as distinct from what they can *do* - a separate 24-subject, 3-tier None/Familiarity/Expertise system with no roll ever, chosen once at chargen and funded by a flat Freebie Pool plus curated Race/Career grants), `races/` (one hand-authored file per race, indexed by `races_overview.md` - see below), `careers.md`, `languages.md`, `progression_&_rewards.md` (the per-level advancement table, plus static prose covering Rewards & Treasure and Itemization). Attributes are STR / DEX / MIND / CHA. The Skill list is fine-grained: per-weapon STR melee Skills (Two-Handed Blades, Cleaving Blades, Hafted Weapons, Polearms, Daggers & Wrestling) with **Fencing Blades** under DEX, **Archery**/**Firearms**/**Thrown** split apart, one Skill per Arcane and Divine school folded into **Thaumaturgy** (Alchemy + Enchanting) rather than a rollable cast, and **Wayfaring** for mounts and route-finding. Combat Attributes: **STR is the whole of melee damage** (finesse blades included; only missiles take no Attribute), **DEX funds Initiative** (`5 + DEX`, static, never rolled) **and the Shot DC a target presents**; the Tempo Pool is sized by equipped gear, not by an Attribute (see the `core/combat/` bullet). Attributes come from a **standard array** per priority letter (A `3, 2, 1, 0` down to E `1, 1, 0, 0`), assigned one number per Attribute with nothing to buy. Attribute growth is a flat **+1 to one Attribute at levels 4, 8 and 12**, hard cap 5. **Attributes may sit below 0** (nothing damages an Attribute, so the only source is a racial -1 on an array 0); **Will never below 0, Slots never below 1** are the only stats that floor instead of following an Attribute down. `Will` is `MIND × 3`. Slots are `6 + STR`, minimum 1. **`core/feats/`, `core/character/races/`, `core/bestiary/`, and `templates/character/character_sheet.html`/`sheet_test.js` have not yet been brought in line with the current combat/magic/equipment rules** and are deferred to their own dedicated pass - see `TODO.md`.
  - `core/character/races/` - `races_overview.md` (hand-authored: General Rules Notes, Race Tiers A-E, and an index of races grouped by Artefolk/Cursed/Mystik/Night-Terror) plus one hand-authored file per race (`aelves.md`, `dwergaz.md`, `humans.md`, `orkhs.md`, `alsahli.md`, `feliids.md`, `skeggs.md`, `tembels.md`, `khoridae.md`, `dzinari.md`, `golems.md`, `tapio.md`, `daemons.md`, `varulf.md`, `strygs.md`). This split (one page per race, mirroring `core/magic/arcane/` and `core/magic/divine/`) exists to make individual races easier to manage and to leave room for per-race lore. **Not yet brought in line with the current combat/magic rules** - a dedicated pass, deliberately deferred; see `TODO.md`.
- `core/combat/` - three files. Read `exchange.md` first - the other two assume it.
  - `exchange.md` - **melee combat entire, and the file that matters.** One resource, the **Tempo Pool** - `+4` baseline plus the equipped weapon's (and shield's) Attacks modifier, every die a flat `1d12`, refilling in full at the **start of the round** (not your own turn) - pays for every attack, every defense, every Opportunity Attack, and Exerting; **there are no Reactions and no free defense of any kind**. **No Attribute sizes the pool** - STR is melee damage only, DEX funds Initiative and Shot DC instead. An Exchange is one attack and its answer, **defender rolls first**: the defender invests any number of dice into a **Parry** (or declines, DC 0) before the attacker invests at all; higher total wins, ties to the defender, **margin 5+ takes an Opening** (Riposte, Disarm, Sunder, Shove, Grapple, or a Feat) - **margin 3 for that weapon's own Signature Opening** (see `weapons.md`). **There are no critical hits anywhere in Ressam** - margin only ever buys an Opening. Every melee hit that lands costs the target a Tempo Die (**Shock**). **Distance & Reach** governs melee range: every weapon carries one of three **Reach** bands (Normal/Reach 1/Reach 2) with no reach-mismatch bonus - Opportunity Attack triggers (entering vs. leaving) and the Reach-2 strike-past-an-intervening-square clause are keyed off Reach. **Initiative** is `5 + DEX`, static, never rolled; Mythic Initiative works as usual on top. **Awareness** is a pure GM fictional call, not a roll or subsystem: an unaware target simply can't Parry the first attack against it (melee) or eats the Shot DC table's `-3` row (ranged), and the ambush is spent after that one attack. **Fear** (a creature's Fear rating triggers a Will-funded resist the first time an enemy tries to close) **and Morale/Nerve** (CHA-funded, `1d12 + CHA` vs. Standard DC 7, tested on set triggers - Unnamed creatures **Break** on a fail, Named characters take Trauma instead and never Break) are documented here but reference Trauma bands in `rest_and_survival.md`. **Exerting**: out of Tempo Dice but still need to Parry, invest a `1d6` and take 1 Trauma instead, capped at 1 Exert die per Parry. Ranged combat is **not** an Exchange (unanswerable, no Opening, no Shock) but **a shot invests Tempo Dice exactly like a melee attack** - take the highest, add Archery/Firearms/Thrown Skill, against a **Shot DC** built off `7 + target's DEX` plus situational modifiers. Its **Loading** ladder covers bows, crossbows, and every firearm, and **Misfire** triggers off the shot's own invested dice (a die at or below the weapon's Misfire score) instead of a separate roll.
  - `combat.md` - action economy (no Reaction row; **Lesser Action** covers quick object interaction), the damage roll (**melee damage is STR for every weapon and Skill without exception; missiles take no Attribute at all**), a *There Are No Critical Hits* section that points at `exchange.md`, and armor's in-fight behavior - **Unarmored reads Dent 0 / Rend 5 like a real armor value**, and firearms get **Armor-Piercing** (a per-hit Dent/Rend reduction, see `weapons.md`) rather than a separate degradation special case.
  - `positioning.md` - the grid, movement, and Cover. Cover is **binary**. Reach 2 is the only threat with a strike-past-an-intervening-square clause.
- `core/magic/` - spellcasting rules split into `laws_of_magic.md` (the in-fiction physics every spell, ritual, and minor working answers to - Five Laws that bound what magic can ever do (no true creation from nothing, no touching past/future, no making/unmaking/growing a soul, no rewriting essential nature by mortal craft, no effect without a thread of sight/touch/sympathy), Three Costs that price what's lawful but costly (Conservation, Signature, Reciprocity), and an Adjudication section for when the spell lists are silent on a player's novel idea - read this before ruling on anything not covered by an existing spell), `magic_overview.md` (universal rules shared by every school), and `minor_magic.md` (small, instinctive Will effects - minimum 1 Will, scaling in scale/duration from there - that fall outside a normal working), with two parallel school trees. MIND is a hard **ceiling on dice committed** to any one cast or resist (never more dice than your MIND), not a modifier; casting commits Will 1-per-die (paid before rolling), rolls that many `1d12`s and takes the **highest**, against a flat DC set by the working's **tier** - Lesser/Common/Greater/Legendary at `5/7/9/11`, the same four numbers `core_rules.md`'s DC ladder uses. A miss still spends the Will. **Push** (spend Trauma, `+1` per point to the highest die, max `+2`) and **Channel** (declared at the end of your prior turn, sets one die to the working's difficulty exactly, Trauma-costed by tier) round out the loop. A targeted working is answered by the defender committing their own Will and rolling to **tie or beat** the caster's highest die - never a Ward, never a flat roll bonus from a Feat. Damage workings ignore armor outright (compare to Dent 0 / Rend 5 regardless of what's worn) and carry no damage type. **Not yet converted: the actual spell lists.** `magic/arcane/*.md` and `magic/divine/*.md` still describe every spell in an older Mana-Cost/flat-modifier/Spell-Attack-vs-Overcome shape - this is deliberately deferred, per-spell design work, not a mechanical find-and-replace; see `TODO.md`.
  - `magic/arcane/` - arcane schools (aeromancy, geomancy, hydromancy, pyromancy, shadowmancy) plus `arcane_overview.md`.
  - `magic/divine/` - divine schools (benediction, cultivation, invocation, necration, subjugation) plus `divine_overview.md`.
- `core/feats/` - feat categories (general, martial, magic, prestige, skill, racial), indexed by `feats_overview.md`. Magic Feats (`magic_feats.md`) are one unified system across all ten schools - no Arcane/Divine split. `feats_overview.md` states the ceiling that keeps prerequisites honest: **no ordinary Feat asks for an Attribute above 2, no Feat asks for more than `+3` ranks in a Skill, and a Prestige Feat may ask for 3 and no more** - because a prerequisite is a level gate in disguise (outside an **A** you start at 2, reach 3 at Level 4, 4 at Level 8), and **a Skill Rank requirement is an Attribute requirement too**, since Rank <= governing Attribute. To make a Feat harder to get, add a second requirement, never a bigger number. No Feat gates above Level 6 for anything but Prestige. `racial_feats.md` holds racial traits strong enough to cost a Feat slot instead of coming free with that race - grouped by race, gated by a `Race: <Name>` prerequisite. **`general_feats.md`, `martial_feats.md`, and `skill_feats.md` are currently empty stub headers**, cleared in preparation for a full rewrite against the current rules; `prestige_feats.md` and `racial_feats.md` have real content but haven't been fully checked against the current magic rules; `feats_overview.md` is short and spot-checked clean but hasn't had a full read-through. See `TODO.md`.
  - `magic_feats.md` - every one of the ten schools uses the same template: a Novice/Adept/Expert/Master **Unlock** tier (gating Lesser/Common/Greater/Legendary difficulty) and a matching **Focus** tier built around **Signature Workings** (a discounted, sometimes free-to-Channel working of your choice) rather than a flat roll bonus. Beyond the one fully worked example (Pyromancy), the full Unlock/Focus/Combination roster for the remaining nine schools is a follow-up content pass - see `TODO.md`.
- `core/equipment/` - `armor.md`, `weapons.md`, `supplies.md`, and `alchemy.md` (crafting oils/bombs/salves from ingredients). Shields (the Shield Table/Descriptions/Guard rules) live in `weapons.md`, not `armor.md` - a shield isn't armor, it's something you actively wield in a hand, and can be swung: a **Shield Bash** attack (1d4 Blunt) uses the **Daggers & Wrestling Skill**, same as an unarmed strike. Shield repair runs through the armor repair rules (shields share durability with armor, no separate track). `weapons.md`'s weapons carry **Reach** (Normal/Reach 1/Reach 2, no reach-mismatch bonus) and one **Signature Opening** per weapon (the specific Opening - Riposte/Disarm/Sunder/Shove/Grapple - that weapon reaches at margin 3 instead of 5). Every weapon prints an **Attacks** modifier (`+1` Light / `-1` Two-Handed / `+0` else) that directly sets Tempo Pool size. Skills: **Hafted Weapons** (Normal Reach) and **Polearms** (Reach 1/2) split off from a single combined Skill; **Archery** (bows and crossbows) and **Firearms** (guns) split off from a single combined Skill; **Thrown** stands alone. **Armor-Piercing** lowers the target's Dent/Rend Lines by its AP value for that one hit. Armor field repair is a function of `rest_and_survival.md`'s Rest & Repair table (a flat `+1` per Field Rest, `+1` or `+2` on a Long Rest with a Smithing check), not tracked in `armor.md` itself. Shields carry an "Against shots" column (a Heater Shield counts as Light cover against a shot at its bearer). Carrying capacity is unified: every suit of armor, shield, and weapon not currently equipped costs **1 Slot**, matching the unified Slots pool (see the `core/character/` bullet). `alchemy.md`'s Vital Oil recipes heal real Wounds at the same Trauma cost as any other healing; only Unwinding removes Trauma - nothing in Alchemy bypasses that. `supplies.md`'s Medical Supplies section includes a Healing Draught entry, and its Chirurgery/Stabilization wording matches Downed. `armor.md`'s Dent/Rend table: Gambeson 5/10, Buff Coat/Mail Shirt/Chain Mail 6/11, Brigandine/Breastplate 7/12, Half-Plate 9/14, Full Plate 11/16 - a 5-point gap between Dent and Rend throughout. `core/bestiary/` has not been touched by any equipment pass - see `TODO.md`.
- `core/exploration/` - travel and downtime layer: `traveling.md` (hex travel, mounts), `downtime.md` (revelry/celebrations), `leadership.md` (followers and cohorts), `dungeon_turns.md` (the 10-minute Turn as the clock for dungeon/site exploration - light tracking and wandering encounters share one party-wide tally), and `stealth_and_light.md` (Stealth and Hiding, Light and Vision - split out of `core_rules.md` and placed here since Light and Vision's burn-duration table already lived in lockstep with `dungeon_turns.md`'s Light Tracker). These feed the core loop directly, not optional flavor: Traveling is how the party reaches a dungeon and burns rations, Leadership is how a growing operation delves deeper without resurfacing, and Downtime is where rewards from delving cycle back into progression.
- `core/setting/` - the world of Ressam itself: `setting_overview.md` (welcome blurb, currency, language, and an index of this chapter - the `races_overview.md`/`feats_overview.md` pattern), `calendar.md` (the seasonal year and its festivals), `history.md` (Age of Mythos, the Sundering, Age of Revival, and the Third Age's wars up to 402 RC), `geography.md` (the Mourning Star and twin moons, the continents, and the Mortal Plane/Fields of Nocturne), `nations.md` (the great powers, the nation relations table, and global/local factions), `religions.md` (the faiths practiced across Ressam), and `heroes.md` (legendary figures). Cross-references a lot with `core/character/races/` - each race's own page covers that race's culture and mythology in more depth than fits here.
- `DESIGN_GUIDE.md` - the yardstick for evaluating any mechanic, item, spell, or rule change. **Read the three subsections under Scarcity before writing any number a player rolls against or has to reach**: they cover why a Feat slot is the cost and the prerequisite is only flavor, why the `1d12 \+ (0-5)` ceiling of 17 has to be checked before setting a DC or a threshold, and why a Ward must not be priced like a Skill Check. They also record the **5th Edition mirror** - Ressam's Attributes map onto 5e's ability *modifiers* almost exactly (2 = a 14-15, 5 = a 20), and 5e's array in modifier terms is nearly our **C**'s, which makes 5e a usable sanity check for numbers of this shape: notably, every ability-score prerequisite in 5e is exactly 13, a \+1, and never higher. Five Design Pillars (Realism as "feel, don't simulate," Elegance, Scarcity, Niches, and Distinction between Martials and Casters), a Prose Style section (table-reference density over teaching text - see the Content conventions section below), followed by Spell Design Guidelines and Feat/Magic Feat Guidelines. Read this before proposing or reviewing changes to `core/magic/`, `core/feats/`, or `core/equipment/` - it's the reasoning behind *why* those files are shaped the way they are, not just what's in them. Several of its Spell Design Guidelines (Creation, Healing, Summoning, Faith, Bypass) are the mechanical/production-side expression of Laws `laws_of_magic.md` states in-fiction (Borrowed Substance, Conservation, the Soul, Reciprocity) - the two files should stay consistent with each other when either changes. Its Spell Design Guidelines and Magic Feat Guidelines sections are flagged stale in-place pending the spell-list conversion pass - see `TODO.md`.
- `tools/` - Python, standalone (stdlib only, no install step). **Currently stale and deliberately left that way:** `combat_engine.py`, `creature_rating.py`, `encounter_rating.py`, and `tempo_sim.py` all model an earlier combat system, and their numbers mean nothing about the current rules. Rebuilding this directory against the current rules is open work; until it is done, do not quote a number from any of these tools. The description below is of what they're meant to do once rebuilt - the computational counterpart to `DESIGN_GUIDE.md`'s qualitative pillars: run these to check whether a mechanical change actually lands the way it reads on paper, rather than eyeballing it. Two independent pieces:
  - `power_score.py` - prices a creature backward through `progression_&_rewards.md`'s own Attribute/Skill/Feat advancement table to find the lowest PC Level that could legally afford it (its "Effective Level"), and reads an XP reward straight off that table - no borrowed CR-style curve, no simulation, just point-budget accounting. Cheap enough to run on every bestiary entry.
  - `combat_engine.py` backs two Monte Carlo rating tools, `creature_rating.py` (1-on-1) and `encounter_rating.py` (N monsters vs. a 4-PC party). **Why this shape, not a raw number or a D\&D-style CR curve:** Ressam PCs don't scale much across Level 1-12, so both tools instead simulate many fights against one fixed reference build, `combat_engine.BASELINE_PC`, and bucket the win rate into a 5-tier rating - **Much Worse / Worse / Equal / Better / Much Better** - that stays meaningful at any Level rather than needing a level-scaled baseline. Run them (`python3 tools/creature_rating.py`, `python3 tools/encounter_rating.py`) after any change to `core/combat/`, `core/equipment/weapons.md` or `armor.md`, or a creature's Attributes/Skills/gear, to see whether the change actually shifted the tier you meant it to. The `bestiary-npc` skill (`.claude/skills/`) already wires both into its Validate stage when generating `core/bestiary/*.md` entries.

- `TODO.md` - planning doc for in-progress, cross-session rework items, when one exists. Only exists while something is actively open; gets deleted once that item is fully resolved and merged, so its absence means "nothing outstanding" rather than an oversight. ## Publishing: GitHub Wiki

The rulebook is published as a GitHub Wiki, auto-synced from `core/` on every push to `main` via `.github/workflows/sync-wiki.yml`: the workflow clones the wiki's own git repo (`<repo>.wiki.git`), mirrors `core/`'s contents into it 1:1 (folder structure preserved, `core/` prefix stripped), and pushes only if something changed. **Never hand-edit wiki pages that correspond to a `core/**/*.md` file** - edits made directly in the wiki UI are silently overwritten on the next sync. Edit `core/` and let CI publish it.

Two pages exist **only** in the wiki repo, not in this one, and *are* meant to be hand-edited directly in the wiki UI: `Home.md` (landing page) and `_Sidebar.md` (persistent nav, shown on every page). The sync workflow explicitly excludes both from its mirror/delete step so it never overwrites them. Both list every chapter grouped by section, in the same order as "Repository structure" above. Local reference copies live at `templates/Home.md` and `templates/_Sidebar.md` for convenience - edit those first, then paste the result into the wiki UI. When a chapter is added, renamed, or removed under `core/`, update both pages (and their local copies) to match.

**GitHub Wiki quirk to know before editing either page:** wiki page URLs/links are flat and basename-only, regardless of which subfolder a file is mirrored into - e.g. `core/magic/arcane/pyromancy.md` is mirrored to `magic/arcane/pyromancy.md` in the wiki repo but renders at `/wiki/pyromancy`, not `/wiki/magic/arcane/pyromancy`. Multi-level nested paths in Markdown links (e.g. `[Pyromancy](magic/arcane/pyromancy)`) 404. Always link to wiki pages by bare filename with no folder prefix and no `.md` extension, e.g. `[Pyromancy](pyromancy)`. This only works because every `core/**/*.md` basename is unique repo-wide - if that ever stops being true, this linking scheme needs to be revisited.

## Content conventions

- Em Dashes (—) and En Dashes (–) are not used, instead use only Hyphens (-).
- Files are exported from Google Docs, so literal Markdown-special characters are backslash-escaped in the source (e.g. `1d12 \+ Skill Ranks \+ Attribute`, `Ressam is not Dungeons \& Dragons`, `Slots \= STR \+ END`). Preserve this escaping style when editing existing text so re-exports/diffs stay clean.
- Formulas and derived stats are given as bolded inline math (e.g. `**Maximum Wounds = Size baseline + Feats**`) rather than code blocks. Follow this pattern for new formulas.
- Section breaks use a horizontal rule (`---`) between major topics within a chapter.
- Tables are used heavily for skill lists, DC tiers, and comparative mechanics (e.g. Arcane vs. Divine casting) - match existing table shapes when adding related content instead of introducing prose where a table already exists for that topic.
- **A `[[Display Text|PageName]]` wiki link inside a table cell breaks the table** - the `|` in the link syntax reads as a column separator to the table parser. Inside a table cell, link with the pipe-free form `[[PageName]]` instead (it displays the bare page name, e.g. `[[exchange]]`) and put whatever display text is needed in the surrounding cell prose - e.g. `see Grappling in [[exchange]]`, not `see [[Grappling|exchange]]`. Outside a table this isn't an issue; `[[Display Text|PageName]]` is fine in ordinary prose.
- Core terminology is reused verbatim across chapters (e.g. `Major Action`, `Ward`, `Dent Line`/`Rend Line`, `Trauma`, `Will`, `Reach`, `Attacks`) - when editing one chapter, check whether the same term/mechanic is defined or referenced elsewhere (`core_rules.md`, `rest_and_survival.md`, `combat.md`, `exchange.md`, `magic_overview.md`) so definitions stay consistent. `AR`, `Evasion`, and `Mana` are retired terms - don't reintroduce them in new text (use `Dent Line`/`Rend Line`, the Tempo Pool, and `Will` respectively).
- The game's tone/scope per `core_rules.md`'s foreword: Ressam is a crunchy, lethal alternative to other TTRPGs, set in a world balanced around an early-1500s baseline - matchlock firearms sit alongside plate armor, magic is feared and regulated. Combat is the heart of the game; everything outside of it is meant to be played loosely, "rulings not rules."
- **Prose style:** `core/` text should read as a table reference, not a teaching text - state each rule once, in the fewest words that remove ambiguity. Cut sentences that explain *why* a rule is shaped the way it is (that belongs in `DESIGN_GUIDE.md`, not `core/`) unless removing the sentence would let a reader reasonably misapply the rule - see `DESIGN_GUIDE.md`'s Prose Style section for the full litmus test and worked examples. Default edge cases to a GM ruling instead of an enumerated sub-case, and prefer tables/bullets over prose past two comparable items. This targets prose density only, not mechanical scope - Ressam's crunch stays; the words around it don't.
- **Don't narrate the editing history of a rule inside `core/`.** No dates, no "was renamed," "used to be," "replaces the old X," or references to any `*_draft.md`/design-log file. State the current rule only; a change's rationale and history belong in `exchange_log.md` and `DESIGN_GUIDE.md`, not the player-facing chapters. The one exception is a plain, non-historical flag on content that is genuinely inconsistent with the current rules and awaiting its own pass (see the `core/feats/`, `core/character/races/`, `core/bestiary/`, and `templates/character/` notes above) - say what's wrong and where the correct version lives, not when or why it fell behind.
